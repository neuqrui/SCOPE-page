01The motivation

An outcome tells only part of the story.

A successful trajectory can contain mistakes. A failed one can contain good decisions. Corrective guidance needs to match the step that actually needs it.

≠

Success ≠ every step is right

In the annotated WebShop trajectories, 29.8% of steps in successful episodes are erroneous; 42.4% of steps in failed episodes are correct.

↗

A skill ≠ guidance for every step

Skill-unrelated steps account for 57.9% of nonzero sampled-token gaps. Applying trajectory-level skills everywhere can propagate off-target signals.

02The approach

Find the error. Focus the learning.

The same policy acts, analyzes its own experience, and learns from step-specific skills. Auxiliary distillation is applied only at selected error steps.

INTERACTIVE METHOD

One policy. A focused learning loop.

Follow one training iteration, from experience to correction.

Conceptual illustration · step selections and lengths are examples, not annotations of the replay episode.

Full method diagram from the paper＋
  1. 01

    Initialize the analyzer

    A lightweight supervised warm-up teaches the policy to locate errors and write corrective skills. External annotation is confined to this offline stage.

  2. 02

    Locate critical errors

    During online RL, the policy analyzes both successful and failed rollouts, assigning each selected error step its own relevant hindsight skill.

  3. 03

    Bound the correction

    A bounded signed teacher–student coefficient preserves reinforcement and suppression while limiting extreme auxiliary update weights.

  4. 04

    Reward efficient success

    A success-relative pairwise length reward favors shorter successful trajectories within the same task group, without a fixed length target.

Training-time hindsight. Ordinary inference.

The learned policy completes tasks without skill conditioning or analyzer calls at evaluation.

05A closer look

          
Beyond the main benchmark.

          
Unseen tasks, optimization diagnostics, and component ablations examine what the complete system learns and how it trains.

        

        

          
01 / Generalization
More unseen tasks solved. Fewer interactions.
On unseen ALFWorld tasks, success rises from 66.4% with GRPO to 89.6% with SCOPE. Average interactions fall from 18.86 to 10.06.

02 / Optimization
Keep extreme gaps in check.
The paper records a sharp student log-probability drop during minibatch optimization. SCOPE bounds the signed distillation coefficient to limit gap-driven amplification.

            
          

          
03 / Interaction efficiency
Encourage shorter successful behavior.
Pairwise rewards compare interaction lengths among successes from the same task group. The plot reports mean response steps during ALFWorld training; lower is better.

            
          

        

        
04 / Component ablations
Each component has a role.
At training step 180, full SCOPE reaches 98.4% ALFWorld success. Removing supervised initialization yields 92.2%; removing the length reward yields 93.0%.

          
        

        
Supplementary analysis: parameter sensitivity+
The default setting is α = 2.5, c = 1, and τlen = 0.1. The paper varies one parameter at a time and reports task-family success at training step 180.
